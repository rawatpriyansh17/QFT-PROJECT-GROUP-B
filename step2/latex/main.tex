\section{Normal modes and the reduced state}
We first test the entanglement calculation using two coupled harmonic oscillators, whose ground state can be obtained exactly. In units $\hbar=1$ and with both oscillator masses set to unity,
\begin{equation}
H=\frac{\bm p^T\bm p}{2}+\frac12\bm x^TK\bm x,\qquad
K=\begin{pmatrix}1&c\\c&2\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Diagonalising $K$ gives
\begin{equation}
\lambda_\pm=\omega_\pm^2=\frac{3\pm\sqrt{1+4c^2}}{2}.
\end{equation}
A stable ground state requires both eigenvalues to be positive. Since $\mathrm{Tr}\,K=3>0$, this is equivalent to $\det K=2-c^2>0$, or $|c|<\sqrt2$, satisfied by $c=0.3,0.6$.
Writing $K=U\,\mathrm{diag}(\omega_+^2,\omega_-^2)U^T$, introduce normal coordinates $\bm y=U^T\bm x$ and momenta $\bm\pi=U^T\bm p$. Then
\begin{equation}
H=\sum_{j=\pm}\left(\frac{\pi_j^2}{2}+\frac{\omega_j^2y_j^2}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
Each normal mode has ground-state wavefunction $\psi_0(y)\propto e^{-\omega y^2/2}$, with $\langle y^2\rangle=1/(2\omega)$ and $\langle\pi^2\rangle=\omega/2$. Transforming back gives
\begin{equation}
X=\langle\bm x\bm x^T\rangle=\tfrac12K^{-1/2},\quad
P=\langle\bm p\bm p^T\rangle=\tfrac12K^{1/2},\quad
\tfrac12\langle\{x_i,p_j\}\rangle=0.
\end{equation}
The normal-mode ground state is a product state, but each normal mode involves both original oscillators when $c\ne0$. Their joint state $\rho_{12}=|\Psi_{12}\rangle\langle\Psi_{12}|$ is pure; tracing out oscillator 2 gives $\rho_1=\mathrm{Tr}_2\rho_{12}$ with covariance
\begin{equation}
V_1=\mathrm{diag}(X_{11},P_{11}),\qquad \nu=\sqrt{\det V_1}=\sqrt{X_{11}P_{11}}.
\end{equation}
Here $\nu=1/2$ describes a pure reduced state, while $\nu>1/2$ indicates mixedness and, because the joint state is pure, entanglement with oscillator 2. Its von Neumann entropy is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{s(\nu)=(\nu+\tfrac12)\ln(\nu+\tfrac12)-(\nu-\tfrac12)\ln(\nu-\tfrac12).}
\end{equation}
Using $0\ln0=0$, we obtain $s(1/2)=0$. Entropy is measured in nats.

For an explicit evaluation, set $d=\sqrt{2-c^2}$ and $t=\sqrt{3+2d}$. The matrix square roots are
\begin{equation}
K^{1/2}=\frac{K+dI}{t},\qquad K^{-1/2}=\frac1{dt}\begin{pmatrix}2+d&-c\\-c&1+d\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\nu=\sqrt{\frac{(2+d)(1+d)}{4d(3+2d)}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Numerical checks and interpretation}
Diagonalisation gives $\nu=0.5028165419863$ and $s=0.0193599304236$ at $c=0.3$. At $c=0$ the oscillators decouple and the entropy vanishes; increasing $c$ to $0.6$ raises it to $0.06727$.

As an independent check, use the ground-state Gaussian wavefunction
\begin{equation}
\psi(\bm x)\propto\exp\!\left(-\tfrac12\bm x^TA\bm x\right),\qquad A=K^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Tracing out oscillator 2 and evaluating the Gaussian integrals gives the reduced purity,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho_1^2=\sqrt{\frac{\det A}{A_{11}A_{22}}}=\frac1{2\nu}.
\end{equation}
Purity is one for a pure state and smaller than one for a mixed state, independently checking the covariance result.

Under a local canonical rescaling $x_1\mapsto bx_1$, $p_1\mapsto p_1/b$, individual variances change, but $X_{11}P_{11}=\nu^2$ and the entropy remain unchanged. The largest numerical change in this product over $b=0.1,0.5,1,2,10$ is $5.551\times10^{-17}$. Thus local squeezing cannot create entanglement between initially decoupled oscillators.
