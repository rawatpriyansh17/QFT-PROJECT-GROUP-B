\section{Legendre transform with derivative mixing}

For one real Fourier sector, the Lagrangian is
\begin{equation}
L_k=\frac{a^2}{2}(q'^2+\sigma'^2)
-\frac{a^2k^2}{2}(q^2+\sigma^2)
-\frac{a^4m^2}{2}\sigma^2+a^3\rho q'\sigma.
\end{equation}

The derivative mixing modifies the canonical momenta, which must be obtained directly from the full Lagrangian:
\begin{equation}
\pi_q=\frac{\partial L_k}{\partial q'}
=a^2q'+a^3\rho\sigma,\qquad
\pi_\sigma=\frac{\partial L_k}{\partial \sigma'}
=a^2\sigma'.
\end{equation}
Inverting these relations gives
\begin{equation}
q'=\frac{\pi_q-a^3\rho\sigma}{a^2},
\qquad \sigma'=\frac{\pi_\sigma}{a^2}.
\end{equation}

The Hamiltonian follows from the Legendre transform
$H_k=\pi_qq'+\pi_\sigma\sigma'-L_k$.
Substituting the velocities and collecting terms gives
\begin{align}
H_k={}&\frac{(\pi_q-a^3\rho\sigma)^2+\pi_\sigma^2}{2a^2}
\nonumber\\
&+\frac{a^2k^2}{2}(q^2+\sigma^2)
+\frac{a^4m^2}{2}\sigma^2.
\end{align}

Expanding the momentum-dependent square makes the mixing contributions explicit:
\begin{equation}
\frac{(\pi_q-a^3\rho\sigma)^2}{2a^2}
=\frac{\pi_q^2}{2a^2}-a\rho\pi_q\sigma
+\frac{a^4\rho^2\sigma^2}{2}.
\end{equation}
Thus, although the mixing in the Lagrangian is linear in $\rho$,
the Hamiltonian contains a quadratic $\rho^2$ contribution.
This arises because the velocity $q'$ itself depends on $\rho$,
and the Legendre transform involves its square. The term must be
retained: it contributes to the spectator-field dynamics and
combines with the mass term to give an effective coefficient
proportional to $m^2+\rho^2$. Dropping it would therefore change
the equations of motion and the subsequent mode evolution.

Hamilton's equations,
$q'=\partial H_k/\partial\pi_q$ and
$\pi_q'=-\partial H_k/\partial q$, together with their
$\sigma$ counterparts, yield
\begin{align}
q'&=\frac{\pi_q-a^3\rho\sigma}{a^2},
&\pi_q'&=-a^2k^2q,\\
\sigma'&=\frac{\pi_\sigma}{a^2},
&\pi_\sigma'&=a\rho\pi_q-a^2k^2\sigma
-a^4(m^2+\rho^2)\sigma.
\end{align}

An important consequence is that an initially vanishing
$\pi_q$ does not imply a vanishing $q'$:
\begin{equation}
\pi_q = 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
q'=-a\rho\sigma,
\end{equation}
which is nonzero for $\rho\sigma\neq0$. Thus, even a
spectator-only initial excitation induces an immediate
evolution of the curvature field through derivative mixing.

\section{Dimensionless equations and an independent formulation}

We introduce dimensionless variables
\begin{equation}
x=-k\eta,\qquad M=\frac{m}{H},\qquad g=\frac{\rho}{H},
\end{equation}
and rescale the two independent mode columns as
\begin{equation}
q_k^b=Hk^{-3/2}Q_b,\qquad
\sigma_k^b=Hk^{-3/2}\Sigma_b.
\end{equation}
Here, \(b=1,2\) labels the two independent mode columns corresponding to the two independent initial quantum mode solutions. For de Sitter expansion, $a=-1/(H\eta)=k/(Hx)$, and
$\partial_\eta=-k\partial_x$. The dimensionless canonical
momenta are defined by
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{q,b}=\frac{H}{k^{3/2}}\pi_{q,b},
\qquad
\Pi_{\sigma,b}=\frac{H}{k^{3/2}}\pi_{\sigma,b}.
\end{equation}
Substituting these definitions into the canonical equations
derived in Section 1 gives
\begin{align}
\partial_x Q&=\frac{g\Sigma}{x}-x^2\Pi_q,
&\partial_x\Pi_q&=\frac{Q}{x^2},\\
\partial_x\Sigma&=-x^2\Pi_\sigma,
&\partial_x\Pi_\sigma&=
\frac{\Sigma}{x^2}
+\frac{(M^2+g^2)\Sigma}{x^4}
-\frac{g\Pi_q}{x}.
\end{align}
Here and below, the mode-column index $b$ is suppressed.

We also construct an independent formulation involving only
the mode functions. Introduce the time variable
\begin{equation}
N=-\ln x,\qquad
\partial_N=-x\partial_x,
\end{equation}
and use dots to denote derivatives with respect to $N$.
As $x$ decreases from large to small values during the
evolution, $N$ increases monotonically.

To eliminate the canonical momenta, the first and third
canonical equations give
\begin{equation}
\dot Q=-g\Sigma+x^3\Pi_q,\qquad
\dot\Sigma=x^3\Pi_\sigma.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\Pi_q=\frac{\dot Q+g\Sigma}{x^3},
\qquad
\Pi_\sigma=\frac{\dot\Sigma}{x^3}.
\label{eq:momentum_reconstruction}
\end{equation}

Differentiating the first relation for $\dot Q$ with respect
to $N$, and using $\dot\Pi_q=-x\partial_x\Pi_q=-Q/x$,
yields
\begin{align}
\ddot Q
&=-g\dot\Sigma-3x^3\Pi_q-x^2Q\\
&=-g\dot\Sigma-3(\dot Q+g\Sigma)-x^2Q.
\end{align}
Thus, the curvature mode satisfies
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\ddot Q+3\dot Q+x^2Q=-g(\dot\Sigma+3\Sigma).
}
\end{equation}

Similarly, differentiating $\dot\Sigma=x^3\Pi_\sigma$
and substituting the canonical equation for $\Pi_\sigma$
gives
\begin{align}
\ddot\Sigma
&=-3\dot\Sigma+x^3\dot\Pi_\sigma\\
&=-3\dot\Sigma-x^2\Sigma-(M^2+g^2)\Sigma
+gx^3\Pi_q\\
&=-3\dot\Sigma-x^2\Sigma-(M^2+g^2)\Sigma
+g(\dot Q+g\Sigma).
\end{align}
The two $g^2\Sigma$ contributions cancel, leaving
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\ddot\Sigma+3\dot\Sigma+(x^2+M^2)\Sigma=g\dot Q.
}
\end{equation}
Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:momentum_reconstruction},
these form the velocity formulation. It evolves only the
two mode functions, with the canonical momenta reconstructed
when required.

To verify the numerical consistency of the two formulations,
we evolve them independently from identical finite-time
initial data using DOP853, with $M=2$, $g=0.1$, $X=100$
and $x_f=10^{-3}$. We compare the resulting complex mode
columns and the entanglement entropy obtained from both
solutions.

The scaled difference between corresponding mode-function
values is defined as
\begin{equation}
\epsilon(u)=\frac{|u_N-u_x|}{1+|u_N|},
\end{equation}
where $u_N$ and $u_x$ denote the results from the velocity
and canonical formulations, respectively. This measure
remains well behaved near zeros of a mode column.

We first use relative and absolute tolerances of
$10^{-10}$ and $10^{-12}$, respectively, and then repeat
the calculation with both tolerances tightened by a factor
of ten. The maximum scaled column difference and maximum
entropy difference obtained at both precision levels are
summarised below:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & Initial tolerances & Tighter tolerances\\
\midrule
Relative tolerance & $10^{-10}$ & $10^{-11}$\\
Absolute tolerance & $10^{-12}$ & $10^{-13}$\\
Maximum scaled column difference
& $2.010\times10^{-7}$ & $2.362\times10^{-8}$\\
Maximum entropy difference
& $5.111\times10^{-7}$ & $6.867\times10^{-8}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Tightening the tolerances reduces the maximum scaled column
difference by approximately a factor of 8.5. The entropy
difference also decreases, demonstrating that the numerical
agreement between the two independently implemented
formulations improves with increased integration precision.
Production precision and initial-state tests are established
in the next steps.
